What is the Sonde Score?

The Sonde Score is a universal 0–1000 compliance index that measures how accessible a website is. It works like a credit score for digital accessibility — higher is better, and the methodology is published openly. The 1000-point scale provides fine-grained differentiation between sites and avoids the misleading "percent correct" interpretation.

The score is normalized per page scanned, so sites of any size can be compared fairly. A 50-page site with 100 findings and a 5-page site with 10 findings of the same severity distribution receive the same score. A score of 500 means the site’s per-page weighted penalty exactly equals the sensitivity parameter.

The Formula

Score = round(1000 × S / (S + P/N))

where S = sensitivity (750), P = Σ(weight × count), N = pages scanned

Each accessibility finding carries a severity weight. The sum of all weighted findings is divided by the number of pages scanned to normalize for site size. A harmonic curve maps the result to a 0–1000 index, ensuring every site gets a meaningful score with no zeros.

Element Compliance Rate (ECR)

The Element Compliance Rate measures what fraction of a website’s UI elements are free of accessibility findings. While the Sonde Score measures the severity-weighted depth of issues, the ECR measures the breadth of compliance across the entire DOM.

ECR = ((Total Elements − Elements with Findings) / Total Elements) × 100

Total Elements is the count of all DOM nodes on each page. Elements with Findings counts unique CSS selectors that have at least one accessibility violation. A page with 500 elements and 12 elements with findings has an ECR of 97.6%. The two metrics together give a complete picture: the Sonde Score tells you how serious the issues are; the ECR tells you how widespread they are.

Section 508 and WCAG

The Section 508 refresh (2017) adopted WCAG 2.0 Level AA as the technical standard for federal agencies. WCAG 2.1 AA is a strict superset of 2.0 AA. Sonde scans against WCAG 2.1 AA and maps findings to both WCAG success criteria and Section 508 provision numbers, so reports show dual references.

VPAT (Voluntary Product Accessibility Template)

Each scanned site includes a downloadable VPAT — the industry-standard format for documenting product accessibility conformance. Published by the Information Technology Industry Council (ITI), the VPAT is widely used in government and enterprise procurement to evaluate whether a product meets accessibility requirements.

Our VPAT reports cover all 50 WCAG 2.1 Level A and Level AA success criteria. Each criterion is rated as Supports (no violations detected), Partially Supports (violations on some pages), Does Not Support (violations across all pages), or Not Evaluated (requires human judgment beyond automated testing). The VPAT also includes the Section 508 crosswalk for each criterion.

OSCAL (Open Security Controls Assessment Language)

Each scanned site includes a downloadable assessment results file in OSCAL format — a standardized, machine-readable data format developed by NIST for expressing security and compliance assessment information.

OSCAL enables automated processing of compliance data across tools and organizations. Government agencies and their contractors increasingly use OSCAL for FedRAMP authorization packages, continuous monitoring, and supply chain risk management. Our OSCAL export follows the Assessment Results model, making scan findings directly consumable by GRC (Governance, Risk, and Compliance) platforms and procurement workflows.
